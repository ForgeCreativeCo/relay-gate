# Relay Gate

An endless survival shooter, tilted top-down, built with Babylon.js. Machines keep coming, sooner and in bigger groups, bosses arrive as you level up, and the run lasts until you go down. There are five arenas, which tell one story in order: Dead City, the Relay Station, the Foundry, 1984 and Origin. The last is locked until you've beaten 1984's boss, and it's the only one that can be won. The maps, models and textures are generated by code; there are no model files, and the only images are the app icons, which a script draws. Sound effects and music are audio files in `sfx/`. The music changes with what's happening: the sci-fi cue on the title screen and in the Relay Station's blackouts, Dead City's own score, drums while Hunter Walkers are about (in Dead City, while a raid is on instead), and the boss track while a Warden or the Crucible is alive. 1984 has its own track, and a faster generated score while the Infiltrator is alive; its rain and its payphone bell are generated in the browser too. Music has its own volume in the menu. Browsers keep sound off until you first click, tap or press a key, so the title screen says so until then.

## Running it on your computer

The game uses JavaScript modules, so it has to be served by a small local web server. Double-clicking `index.html` won't work (browsers block modules opened straight from disk).

Pick one:

- **Python** (installed on most Macs and Linux machines): open a terminal in this folder and run
  `python3 -m http.server 8000`
  then visit http://localhost:8000
- **VS Code**: install the "Live Server" extension, right-click `index.html`, choose "Open with Live Server".
- **Node**: `npx serve .`

You need an internet connection the first time, because Babylon.js and the fonts load from a CDN.

## Installing it

Relay Gate is a PWA: once it is online (GitHub Pages works), you can install it from the browser and it opens fullscreen, like an app, with its own icon. The title screen has an **Install** button wherever the browser can install it (Chrome and Edge on a computer, and Android): it opens the browser's install dialog. On an iPhone or iPad the button explains how: **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen** in Safari. Chrome and Edge also put an install icon at the right of the address bar. Once you have played it online, it starts without a connection too. Inside Claude none of this applies: the game runs as an artifact there.

The manifest's `id` is `/relay-gate/`. Every project on forgecreativeco.github.io shares one site, and browsers read an app's `id` from the site root, so a bare `./` would give every project the same id and the browser would treat them as one app ("Open in app" on another project's page). Give each project its own, e.g. `/nomad-horizon/`.

## How a run plays

Every run opens with the arena's intro: the camera sweeps over what matters there (the gate and the survivors' camp; the leech conduits and the breaker; the fabricators, the slag, the tunnels and the dispatch gate; a payphone, the strip and its shops; the lab, the find and her desk) while Mara says what to do about it, then settles behind you. The first time you see an arena's intro you watch it through; after that any key, click or tap skips it. The shots and Mara's lines are `intro` in each arena's file.

You start with Mara and the starter rifle. Machines arrive from past the edge of the view (and some step out of the gate), sooner and in bigger groups as the clock runs: drones and husks first, Hunter Walkers from two and a half minutes. Going down ends the run and shows how long you lasted, your kills and your best time. Every run starts again from nothing; only your best run is kept.

A horn sounds when a group of machines arrives. Groups come every few seconds once a run is going, so it sounds at most once every twenty seconds (`horn` in the block at the top of `js/survival.js`).

Kills give XP (Mara's count too): 2 for a drone, 3 for a husk, 12 for a walker. Each level stops the game and offers three upgrades, picked at random from fourteen; choose one with a click, a tap or the 1, 2 and 3 keys. Each card says whose it is (weapon, you or Mara), how many times you have taken it, what it comes to with this pick, and whether a boss arrives after it. They cover your gun (damage, fire rate, critical hits, range, healing on kills), you (health, regeneration, armour, speed, dodge) and Mara (her damage, her health and how soon she gets back up), and they last for the run. The XP values, the cost of each level and the upgrades themselves are at the top of `js/survival.js`.

Bosses come with your level. Every third level a Hunter Walker arrives a few seconds after you pick your upgrade, and every tenth level it is the arena's big boss instead, with its health bar, its phases and the machines it calls in: the Warden in Dead City and the Relay Station, the Crucible in the Foundry, the Infiltrator in 1984. (Origin's boss, the first machine, comes when the lab says, not with a level.) They step out of the gate when it is in reach. While a big boss is alive the ordinary groups come more slowly; when it goes down it drops its machine-forged loot. Each one after the first is tougher. Walkers also start turning up with the ordinary groups from six minutes in.

Mara is the medic, in the three arenas she can follow you into (not 1984 or Origin, which are in the past: there, going down ends the run, her upgrades aren't offered, and what heals you is a payphone or a first-aid cabinet). When you are under 70% health and within five metres of her she patches you up for 25 health, once every ten seconds. When you go down she comes for you: she has twelve seconds to reach you, and two and a half seconds beside you gets you back up on half health, with a moment before anything can hurt you. She can do that once a minute (her line on the HUD counts it down). If she is down herself, or has used her revive too recently, going down ends the run. She still shoots, and you can still get her up by standing next to her. Two of the level-up upgrades are for her medic work: bigger heals, and healing and reviving more often. Her numbers are in `MEDIC` in `js/actors.js`.

Loot drops from kills: weapons and armour go in the pack (24 slots), and scrap piles go straight to your scrap. In the pack, pick an item to equip it or scrap it, or use **Scrap all** to turn everything in the pack into scrap at once (what you have equipped stays). It asks first, says how much scrap it comes to, and warns you if any of it was better than what you have on.

Scrap builds a squad of your own: three drones and two husks, together, for 100 scrap. The scrap meter under your level fills as you pick scrap up and scrap loot. When it's full, press B, or click or tap the meter, and the squad is built around you at once. They glow cyan where the enemy's glow red, stay near you, fight whatever comes, and draw fire: the drones from the air, the husks up close. You can't build again until every one of them has been destroyed (the meter counts what's left); scrap keeps adding up in the meantime. No walkers. Their kills count for XP. The cost, the squad and its toughness are `build` in the block at the top of `js/survival.js`.

When a run ends, the card shows how long you lasted, the level you reached, machines, walkers and big bosses destroyed (and, in the Foundry, fabricators shut down and machines the menders rebuilt), how often Mara got you back up, and every upgrade you took. From the card you can run again or switch to any other arena that's open. **End run** in the menu ends a run on the spot.

## The arenas

**Dead City** (region 01): you start in the gate plaza of a ruined, machine-held city, with the relay gate stuck open behind you. The line under the clock shows how the survivors' camp and the gate stand, and when the next raid is due.

- **The camps.** A few survivors sit round the fire at the rail yard camp, just south of the plaza. Every two and a half minutes (the first after two) the horn sounds low and a raid steps out of the gate and marches on the camp: a group one bigger than usual, there for the camp, though it will turn on you if you get in its way. A marker shows the camp and its health while a raid is on, and the drums play. Beat a raid off and the survivors leave you supplies by the fire (scrap and walker-grade loot); while the camp stands, resting by its fire heals you, and the camp mends itself between raids. If its health runs out it burns and the survivors fall back to the outpost, far to the south, where the raids follow them. Clear the rail yard camp of machines and stand in it for 8 seconds to retake it, and they move back up. If the outpost falls too, they're gone: no more raids, but no more supplies either, and every group comes a fifth sooner.
- **The gate.** Stand at the console beside the gate for 8 seconds to shut it (the gate sends three machines at you as you start). For a minute nothing comes through it, groups take 40% longer to come, and no raid can set out; then it opens again (a marker counts down the last ten seconds), and it can't be shut again for 30 seconds.

Only raiders go for a camp; everything else is after you.

**The Relay Station** (region 02): indoors, with every door open. Switch to it on the title screen, or add `?region=2` to the address. The station is four rooms in a loop: the gate room, the service corridor, the cooling hall, and a freight bay that runs south of all three and joins the gate room to the hall through two blast doors wide enough for a walker. Walls between you and the camera sink out of the way. Machines come in through the gate, out of the vents in the corridor's alcoves and from the edges of the hall and the bay. It's the Foundry's front office. Every minute or so the power fails for about 35 seconds: emergency lights only, the blast doors seal either the cooling hall or the gate room off from the rest, and the machines come faster until it is back. You can do something about it:

- **Power leeches.** Fifteen seconds before each failure an alarm sounds and a leech, a Foundry-made parasite, clamps onto one of six power conduits (always the same six, on the north and east walls) and starts draining it. The lights in that part of the station stutter, and a marker shows where it is and how long is left. Destroy it in time and that failure doesn't happen.
- **The breaker.** In a blackout, stand at the breaker in the cooling hall's north-east corner for 3 seconds to bring the power back early. A marker points to it while it's dark.
- **Door panels.** When the blast doors seal you out of the cooling hall (or into the gate room), the panel by the corridor's hall door (or the one by the gate room's door to the corridor) forces that door open after 3 seconds.
- **Vents.** Stand on one of the corridor's two floor vents for 4 seconds to weld it shut: no husks come out of it for 90 seconds, and then it bursts open again.

Things that take a while to use (the breaker, the panels, the vents) are worked at by standing beside them after pressing E or tapping Use; a ring fills on the ground, and stepping away stops it.

**The Foundry** (region 03): where the machines are made. Pick it on the title screen, or add `?region=3` to the address. One big roofless casting hall: tall walls to the north and east, a low parapet on the camera's side, and the gate in the middle of the north wall. Off it are two tight spaces, each under three metres across, where walkers and the Crucible can't follow you; their walls sink while you're in them, as the station's do:

- **The assembly line**, a service tunnel behind the north wall, through a doorway at each end. A conveyor of half-built husks runs along it, and crates squeeze the walkway. Husks lie slumped along it and wake as you pass (a fresh one is put down out of sight every 40 seconds or so, up to four).
- **The slag drains**, a culvert behind the south wall, through three doorways. Each channel empties into its half of it (west or east of the middle doorway): two seconds after a channel pours, that half floods too. Husks lie in wait here as well.

It plays at the same pressure as the city, with three things of its own:

- **Fabricators.** Machines come out of five fabricators built into the walls (and out of the gate). With a mouse, auto-aim leaves them alone, so you choose when to go for one: point at its glowing core. On a phone, where there is nothing to point with, auto-aim takes the nearest one in range whenever no machine is (or tap one to lock on). A destroyed fabricator drops scrap, the groups come more slowly for each one that is down, and it starts up again after 50 seconds. Three of them (both northern ones, and the southern one) have a **kill switch** behind them, in the tunnels: walk up to it and press E (or tap Use) while the fabricator is running, and it shuts down for 100 seconds instead.
- **Slag channels.** Two channels run the length of the hall from the furnaces in the north wall. About every 26 seconds one of them pours: a warning, then five seconds when anything standing in it burns, machines included (walkers and the Crucible take it in the legs and treads). Drones and menders fly over it, and Mara keeps out of it.
- **Menders.** Small yellow repair droids that come with the groups from the first minute. A mender settles beside the wreck of a husk or a walker and welds it back together, and the machine gets up on 60% health (worth XP again, but it drops nothing). It holds still while it works.

The big boss here is **the Crucible**, a tracked forge far bigger than the Warden. Its parts are its cannons, its slag mortar, its hangar and its treads. It shells you with its twin cannons and lobs slag that leaves burning pools. Once it is hurt, its hangar launches drones and menders, and it vents its furnace at anything that gets close. In its last phase it rams: a red lane lights up on the ground first.

Against the east wall stands **the dispatch gate**, with finished husks riding a conveyor into it. It's where what the Foundry makes is sent: not somewhere else, some time else. Once the Crucible is under 30% health the gate loses its hold every few seconds, and for a moment its ring shows where it leads: neon, a wet street, rain. It's scenery; nothing comes out of it.

**1984** (region 04): where the machines are sent. Pick it on the title screen, or add `?region=4` to the address. A strip mall at night in the rain: an arcade, a video store and a TV shop along the north side of a parking lot full of cars, a diner beyond them, a gas station to the east, a road along the south. It's the only arena that hasn't been wrecked. Everything tall stands to the north and east; the gas station's canopy fades when you walk in behind it. The arcade is the one building you can go into, through a front door and a side door that are both too narrow for a walker; its walls sink while you're inside, and its rows of cabinets are cover. Mara isn't here: the radio doesn't carry across time. Three things pull you across the lot:

- **Time gates.** Nothing walks in from the edge of the view. Each group arrives through a time gate, a ball of lightning that opens at one of thirteen spots (never the same one twice running) and takes ten seconds to charge, down to six by nine minutes in. Up to three can be open at once, each with a marker counting it down. Stand in one for a second and a half (E, or tap Use) and it collapses: nothing comes through, and you get about 60% of the XP the group was worth and a little scrap. Miss it and the group steps out. Walkers and the big boss arrive the same way, in a flash, with no warning. A walker never arrives inside the arcade, which it could not get out of.
- **The town.** Four shops have a door the machines can go for: the video store, the TV shop, the diner and the gas station. Four times in ten, what steps out of a time gate heads for the nearest shop instead of for you (it turns on you if you get close). Every blow on a door adds to the town's panic, shown under the clock; the door's alarm lamp flashes and a marker points at it. Panic settles once the shops have been left alone for ten seconds, and the windows of the houses behind the shops come on as it rises. At 100% the town is awake and stays awake: every group comes a quarter sooner and one bigger for the rest of the run, and nothing bothers with the shops any more.
- **Payphones.** Every 50 seconds or so (the first after 35) one of six payphones rings for 22 seconds, somewhere away from you, with a marker. Answer it: under 70% health Mara talks you to a first-aid kit (half your health back), otherwise to supplies (scrap and walker-grade loot).

The big boss is **the Infiltrator**: one machine in a long coat with half a face, built to pass for a person. It doesn't rush you. It walks, always knowing where you are (faster when it's far off), and it fits through the arcade's doors. With a clear line it stops and fires a scatter of bolts from the gun in its right arm; up close it swings. Shoot the arm off and it can only close in; shoot its legs out and it limps. At each new phase it drops to a knee for a couple of seconds, taking more damage, and the first time the coat burns away. Beating it once opens Origin.

**Origin** (region 05): where it started, and the one arena that can be won. It's a dark slot on the title screen until you've beaten the Infiltrator once (`?region=5` opens it regardless). The research lab where what was found in 1984 was taken apart: a lobby, a corridor, offices, a mainframe hall, a clean room, and a central lab with the find opened up on a rig under lights. White walls, beige terminals, green screens. Machines arrive in a flash at the edges of the rooms. Mara isn't here either, but she can talk to you (the find is a relay), and this is where it comes out that the lab's work was hers.

- **Terminals.** Six of them hold the research. Stand at one for 6 seconds to wipe it, and something is gone from the future for good: flight control (drones stop coming), frame assembly (husks), heavy chassis (Hunter Walkers), repair routines (menders), ordnance, and core logic (everything that arrives has 35% less health). The lab gets weaker as you go, the reverse of everywhere else. A marker points to the nearest one that's left.
- **The lab fights back.** Each wipe wakes the two nearest prototypes (pale husks, weaker than the real thing, lying by their benches; they also wake if you walk past) and drops two of the four security shutters for 18 seconds.
- **The first machine.** Five seconds after the third wipe, the find gets up off its rig. It fights with a part of everything copied from it: a scatter of bolts, a husk's lunge, a walker's stomp, the Warden's missiles, drones from its back, and it welds itself back together when left alone. Each terminal also takes one of those away from it (frame assembly its lunge, heavy chassis its stomp, ordnance its missiles, flight control its drones, repair routines its mending; core logic makes it take 35% more damage), whether you wipe it before the fight or during it.
- **Winning.** Wipe all six and destroy the first machine, in either order, and the run is won. The ending plays, and the card gives your time. The record kept here is your fastest win.
- **First-aid cabinets and dictaphones.** Four cabinets on the walls each give back 45% of your health, and refill after 75 seconds. Three dictaphones play what she recorded at the time.

The company's name on the lobby wall and on her badge is a placeholder (`COMPANY` at the top of `js/region5.js`).

The numbers that set the pressure (time between groups, group size, how many machines at once, when walkers start) are in one block at the top of `js/survival.js`. An arena can replace any of them: the station's, and the timing of its power failures, are at the bottom of `js/region2.js`; the Foundry's (fabricators, slag, menders, the dispatch gate) are in `js/region3.js`, and the Crucible's at the top of `js/crucible.js`; 1984's (time gates, panic, payphones) are near the top of `js/region4.js`, and the Infiltrator's at the top of `js/infiltrator.js`; Origin's (terminals, shutters, prototypes) are near the top of `js/region5.js`, and the first machine's at the top of `js/firstmachine.js`.

## Saving

Each arena keeps your best run (time, and the level you reached in it) and your settings, and shows the record on the title screen. Origin keeps your fastest win instead. 1984's save also remembers that you've beaten the Infiltrator, which is what the title screen looks for before it opens Origin. Inside Claude it saves to your account, with one record per arena. Run anywhere else, it saves to that browser's local storage instead. **Clear best run** in the menu wipes the record for the arena you're in.

## Controls

Right-click to walk somewhere (hold to keep steering) · hold left-click to fire: the gun locks on to the nearest threat in range by itself (point at an enemy, or a part of a walker, to aim there instead; with nothing in range it fires toward the cursor) · guns never need reloading · S stop · T next part · Space dodge toward the cursor · scroll wheel (or Q) swap weapons · I pack · B build machines from scrap · C tell Mara to follow or hold · 1 2 3 pick an upgrade · M mute · Esc menu. On a phone the game plays in landscape: drag your left thumb anywhere on the left side for a joystick, and hold Fire to shoot (it locks on to the nearest threat by itself, or tap an enemy to lock on to it). Tap your weapon (bottom centre) to swap between primary and secondary. A Dodge button appears on screen, and the scrap meter is the Build button.

### The last two arenas

Built, as described under The arenas; what follows is the plan they were built from, kept for what's still open in it. Not done yet: the company's name (still a placeholder); more recorded sound for 1984 (it has its music now, but the Infiltrator's score, the rain and the payphone bell are generated stand-ins, and there's no CRT hum beyond a mains hum); the cut to Dead City at the very end (the ending says it in words); and nothing has been tuned by playing. Two things came out differently from the plan: the first machine gets up after three wipes, not after all six, so each further wipe weakens it while you fight it; and Origin's machines arrive in a flash at the edges of rooms, without 1984's charging gates.

The arenas tell one story, in order: **Dead City** (the ruins) → **the Relay Station** (the Foundry's front office) → **the Foundry** (where the machines are made) → **1984** (where they're sent) → **Origin** (where Mara made them, so where you unmake them). Each arena has its own verb: hold, restore, destroy, protect, erase.

**Tying in the Foundry.** The relay gates don't just send machines somewhere, they send them back in time. Things to add to the Foundry to set this up:

- **A dispatch gate** in the Foundry, with machines loading into it. A line for Mara's intro: "And that's where they go. Not *where*. *When*."
- **A time bleed on the Crucible.** When the Crucible is badly hurt, the dispatch gate flickers, and for a moment you glimpse neon and rain through the ring.
- **Why they go back.** The machines are sent to 1984 to make sure the Foundry gets built. Something from the future is found there and becomes the seed of everything, so stopping the arrivals matters.

**1984** (region 04, second to last). An American strip mall at night in 1984: an arcade, a video rental store, a diner, a gas station, a parking lot full of period cars, wet asphalt, sodium streetlights and buzzing neon in magenta and cyan. It's the only arena that hasn't been destroyed yet. The machines come through time gates.

- **Stop the arrivals.** Small temporary time gates open around town, with lightning, a sphere of light and a scorched ring on the pavement. Shut one down while it's still charging and nothing comes through. Arrive late and you fight what stepped out. The gates open in different places, so you can't camp them.
- **Keep the town asleep.** Townspeople stay indoors. Machines that break into buildings fill a panic meter, and a full one makes the rest of the run harder (more machines, coming faster). Like the fallen camps in Dead City, it doesn't end the run.
- **Payphones.** The radio doesn't carry across time, so Mara can only reach you through a ringing payphone. Answer one to get her heal or a supply drop. Payphones ring in different places, so the town pulls you three ways at once: the gates, the buildings and the phones.
- **The arcade** is the tight space, with cabinets for cover.
- **Boss: the infiltrator.** A single hunter that tracks you across the map instead of rushing you. Beating it once unlocks Origin.
- **Sound:** a synth and drum-machine score, CRT hum, and phones ringing in the distance.

**Origin** (region 05, the finale). The research lab where the machine found in 1984 was taken apart, a few years later in the 80s. Clean white corridors, beige terminals, green-phosphor monitors, raised floors with cable runs, clean rooms behind glass and a mainframe hall with tape reels. It's sterile and corporate, the opposite of the Foundry: the machines here aren't made yet, they're being designed. In the central lab, **the find** lies opened up on a rig under lights. Whiteboards and screens show sketches of things you've fought all game: husks, walkers, the Crucible. Prototypes stand half-built on benches.

- **Locked until you beat 1984's boss once.** It shows as a dark slot on the title screen until then. The other four arenas stay open.
- **A run you can win.** It still plays like survival (levels, upgrades, groups getting stronger), but it ends: wipe every terminal and kill the first machine and the run is won. The score is your clear time.
- **Wipe the research.** Terminals around the lab hold pieces of the work. Wipe one by channelling at it, like the gate console. Each wipe also removes something from the future, such as an enemy type that stops coming or a boss that can't appear. The world gets weaker as you go, the reverse of every other arena.
- **The lab fights back.** The prototypes on the benches wake up, crude and unfinished but the first of their kind. Security seals doors, as the Station's blast doors do.
- **Boss: the first machine.** The find wakes up on its rig. It's the oldest machine and the one that started everything, and it fights with bits of every enemy you've met.
- **The name.** "Origin" on the title screen. Inside, the lab carries an invented company's branding (logo, signs, ID badges). The company isn't named yet. Placeholders are Meridian Systems or Halvorsen Labs; check that the name doesn't collide with anything real, and don't echo Cyberdyne.

**The twist: Mara built it.** In the 80s, Mara was the young researcher who took the find apart. Everything in the future came from her work. That's why she knows every arena, where the kill switches are and where the breaker is: in a sense she made them.

- **Origin's intro** is where it comes out. The camera sweeps the lab, and her lines change from orders to confession: "That's my desk. That's my handwriting on the board."
- **During the run,** her old badge photo is on a door, her voice is on a dictaphone log, and her name is on the terminals you're wiping. You're erasing her life's work, and she's the one asking you to.
- **The ending.** The first machine is dead and the last terminal is wiped. The radio goes quiet. Then a phone rings, the way she reached you in 1984. A young voice answers, one you almost recognise. She never makes the discovery, so she never becomes the Mara who guided you. You saved the world by unmaking the person who saved you. Cut to Dead City with the sky clear and the gate dark.

### Things to watch

- **How many enemies at once.** Every machine is a fully modelled, individually animated actor with its own materials and shadows. Dozens on screen is fine; hundreds (a Vampire Survivors-style swarm) would mean rebuilding how enemies are drawn and updated. Plan for fewer, tougher enemies.
- **Balance is the main job.** Spawn rates, the difficulty ramp and boss timing are the game, and they get tuned by playing, not up front.
- **Loot piles up on the ground.** Scrap and items are only picked up when you walk over them, so a long run leaves the ground covered in them.
- **Walkers need room.** They walk round cover instead of through it, on a map of the ground that leaves space for something their size. Where no gap is wide enough (the station's 3 m corridor) there is no way through for them; in the station they go round by the freight bay.
- **Drones indoors.** Outdoors they fly over everything. Indoors they go through the doorways like anything on foot, and are held to a person's width so they can cross from room to room, which lets their rotors clip a wall now and then.
