# Relay Gate

An endless survival shooter, tilted top-down, built with Babylon.js. Machines keep coming, sooner and in bigger groups, bosses arrive as you level up, and the run lasts until you go down. The maps, models and textures are generated by code; there are no model files, and the only images are the app icons, which a script draws. Sound effects are audio files in `sfx/`.

## Running it on your computer

The game uses JavaScript modules, so it has to be served by a small local web server. Double-clicking `index.html` won't work (browsers block modules opened straight from disk).

Pick one:

- **Python** (installed on most Macs and Linux machines): open a terminal in this folder and run
  `python3 -m http.server 8000`
  then visit http://localhost:8000
- **VS Code**: install the "Live Server" extension, right-click `index.html`, choose "Open with Live Server".
- **Node**: `npx serve .`

You need an internet connection the first time, because Babylon.js and the fonts load from a CDN.

## Installing it

Relay Gate is a PWA: once it is online (GitHub Pages works), you can install it from the browser and it opens fullscreen, like an app, with its own icon. In Chrome or Edge on a computer, use the install button in the address bar. On Android, use **Install app** or **Add to Home screen** in the browser menu. On an iPhone or iPad, use **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen** in Safari. Once you have played it online, it starts without a connection too. Inside Claude none of this applies: the game runs as an artifact there.

## How a run plays

You start with Mara and the starter rifle. Machines arrive from past the edge of the view (and some step out of the gate), sooner and in bigger groups as the clock runs: drones and husks first, Hunter Walkers from two and a half minutes. Going down ends the run and shows how long you lasted, your kills and your best time. Every run starts again from nothing; only your best run is kept.

A horn sounds when a group of machines arrives. Groups come every few seconds once a run is going, so it sounds at most once every twenty seconds (`horn` in the block at the top of `js/survival.js`).

Kills give XP (Mara's count too): 2 for a drone, 3 for a husk, 12 for a walker. Each level stops the game and offers three upgrades, picked at random from fourteen; choose one with a click, a tap or the 1, 2 and 3 keys. Each card says whose it is (weapon, you or Mara), how many times you have taken it, what it comes to with this pick, and whether a boss arrives after it. They cover your gun (damage, fire rate, critical hits, range, healing on kills), you (health, regeneration, armour, speed, dodge) and Mara (her damage, her health and how soon she gets back up), and they last for the run. The XP values, the cost of each level and the upgrades themselves are at the top of `js/survival.js`.

Bosses come with your level. Every third level a Hunter Walker arrives a few seconds after you pick your upgrade, and every tenth level it is the Warden instead, with its health bar, its phases and the drones and husks it calls in. They step out of the gate when it is in reach. While a Warden is alive the ordinary groups come more slowly; when it goes down it drops its machine-forged loot. Each Warden after the first is tougher. Walkers also start turning up with the ordinary groups from six minutes in.

Mara is the medic. When you are under 70% health and within five metres of her she patches you up for 25 health, once every ten seconds. When you go down she comes for you: she has twelve seconds to reach you, and two and a half seconds beside you gets you back up on half health, with a moment before anything can hurt you. She can do that once a minute (her line on the HUD counts it down). If she is down herself, or has used her revive too recently, going down ends the run. She still shoots, and you can still get her up by standing next to her. Two of the level-up upgrades are for her medic work: bigger heals, and healing and reviving more often. Her numbers are in `MEDIC` in `js/actors.js`.

Loot drops from kills: weapons and armour go in the pack (24 slots), and scrap piles go straight to your scrap. In the pack, pick an item to equip it or scrap it, or use **Scrap all** to turn everything in the pack into scrap at once (what you have equipped stays). It asks first, says how much scrap it comes to, and warns you if any of it was better than what you have on.

Scrap builds a squad of your own: three drones and two husks, together, for 100 scrap. The scrap meter under your level fills as you pick scrap up and scrap loot. When it's full, press B, or click or tap the meter, and the squad is built around you at once. They glow cyan where the enemy's glow red, stay near you, fight whatever comes, and draw fire: the drones from the air, the husks up close. You can't build again until every one of them has been destroyed (the meter counts what's left); scrap keeps adding up in the meantime. No walkers. Their kills count for XP. The cost, the squad and its toughness are `build` in the block at the top of `js/survival.js`.

When a run ends, the card shows how long you lasted, the level you reached, machines, walkers and Wardens destroyed, how often Mara got you back up, and every upgrade you took. From the card you can run again or switch to the other arena. **End run** in the menu ends a run on the spot.

## The arenas

**Dead City** (region 01): you start in the gate plaza of a ruined, machine-held city, with the relay gate stuck open behind you.

**The Relay Station** (region 02): indoors, with every door open. Switch to it on the title screen, or add `?region=2` to the address. The station is four rooms in a loop: the gate room, the service corridor, the cooling hall, and a freight bay that runs south of all three and joins the gate room to the hall through two blast doors wide enough for a walker. Walls between you and the camera sink out of the way. Machines come in through the gate, out of the vents in the corridor's alcoves and from the edges of the hall and the bay. Every minute or so the power fails for about twenty seconds: emergency lights only, the blast doors seal either the cooling hall or the gate room off from the rest, and the machines come faster until it is back.

The numbers that set the pressure (time between groups, group size, how many machines at once, when walkers start) are in one block at the top of `js/survival.js`. An arena can replace any of them: the station's, and the timing of its power failures, are at the bottom of `js/region2.js`.

## Saving

Each arena keeps your best run (time, and the level you reached in it) and your settings, and shows the record on the title screen. Inside Claude it saves to your account, with one record per arena. Run anywhere else, it saves to that browser's local storage instead. **Clear best run** in the menu wipes the record for the arena you're in.

## Controls

Right-click to walk somewhere (hold to keep steering) · hold left-click to fire: the gun locks on to the nearest threat in range by itself (point at an enemy, or a part of a walker, to aim there instead; with nothing in range it fires toward the cursor) · guns never need reloading · S stop · T next part · Space dodge toward the cursor · scroll wheel (or Q) swap weapons · I pack · B build machines from scrap · C tell Mara to follow or hold · 1 2 3 pick an upgrade · M mute · Esc menu. On a phone the game plays in landscape: drag your left thumb anywhere on the left side for a joystick, and hold Fire to shoot (it locks on to the nearest threat by itself, or tap an enemy to lock on to it). Tap your weapon (bottom centre) to swap between primary and secondary. A Dodge button appears on screen, and the scrap meter is the Build button.

## Plans

The campaign (a level-by-level run through Dead City, with the Rust Dogs raiders, checkpoints and objectives) was retired in favour of survival, which stands on its own. It is in the git history if any of it is wanted back, e.g. the raiders as an enemy type.

### Next

1. **WASD movement on desktop.** Right-click to walk is awkward for dodging round a crowd. Phones already use a joystick.
2. **Something that carries over between runs** (gear, unlocks or a starting choice), once the core run is fun on its own.
3. **More arenas.** The wall generator and power zones make indoor arenas quick to lay out.
4. **Tuning.** Spawn rates, the difficulty ramp, boss timing, upgrade values and the scrap costs are all first guesses.

### Things to watch

- **How many enemies at once.** Every machine is a fully modelled, individually animated actor with its own materials and shadows. Dozens on screen is fine; hundreds (a Vampire Survivors-style swarm) would mean rebuilding how enemies are drawn and updated. Plan for fewer, tougher enemies.
- **Balance is the main job.** Spawn rates, the difficulty ramp and boss timing are the game, and they get tuned by playing, not up front.
- **Loot piles up on the ground.** Scrap and items are only picked up when you walk over them, so a long run leaves the ground covered in them.
- **Walkers need room.** They walk round cover instead of through it, on a map of the ground that leaves space for something their size. Where no gap is wide enough (the station's 3 m corridor) there is no way through for them; in the station they go round by the freight bay.
- **Drones indoors.** Outdoors they fly over everything. Indoors they go through the doorways like anything on foot, and are held to a person's width so they can cross from room to room, which lets their rotors clip a wall now and then.

### Still open

- **Where a run starts.** Dead City starts in the gate plaza and the station in the gate room, both with the gate stuck open. Neither has been compared with anywhere else.
- **Does Mara still shoot,** or only heal and revive? For now she does all three.
- **What carries over between runs,** if anything, and how much.
