# Relay Gate

A tilted top-down shooter built with Babylon.js. The map, models and textures are generated by code; there are no image or model files. Sound effects are audio files in `sfx/`.

Right now it plays as a level: Region 01, Dead City. It is turning into a survival game; see [Where it's heading](#where-its-heading-survival) below.

## Running it on your computer

The game uses JavaScript modules, so it has to be served by a small local web server. Double-clicking `index.html` won't work (browsers block modules opened straight from disk).

Pick one:

- **Python** (installed on most Macs and Linux machines): open a terminal in this folder and run
  `python3 -m http.server 8000`
  then visit http://localhost:8000
- **VS Code**: install the "Live Server" extension, right-click `index.html`, choose "Open with Live Server".
- **Node**: `npx serve .`

You need an internet connection the first time, because Babylon.js and the fonts load from a CDN.

## Survival (first version)

Press **Survival** on the title screen, or add `?mode=survival` to the address, e.g. http://localhost:8000/?mode=survival. You start in the gate plaza with Mara and the starter rifle. Machines arrive from past the edge of the view (and some step out of the gate), sooner and in bigger groups as the clock runs: drones and husks first, Hunter Walkers from two and a half minutes. Going down ends the run and shows how long you lasted, your kills and your best time. Every run starts again from nothing; only your best time is kept, in this browser, in its own slot that never touches your Dead City save.

Kills give XP (Mara's count too): 2 for a drone, 3 for a husk, 12 for a walker. Each level stops the game and offers three upgrades, picked at random from twelve; choose one with a click, a tap or the 1, 2 and 3 keys. They cover your gun (damage, fire rate, critical hits, range, healing on kills), you (health, regeneration, armour, speed, dodge) and Mara (her damage, her health and how soon she gets back up), and they last for the run. The XP values, the cost of each level and the upgrades themselves are at the top of `js/survival.js`.

Bosses come with your level. Every third level a Hunter Walker arrives a few seconds after you pick your upgrade, and every tenth level it is the Warden instead, with its health bar, its phases and the drones and husks it calls in. They step out of the gate when it is in reach. While a Warden is alive the ordinary groups come more slowly; when it goes down it drops its machine-forged loot. Each Warden after the first is tougher. Walkers also start turning up with the ordinary groups from six minutes in.

Mara is the medic. When you are under 70% health and within five metres of her she patches you up for 25 health, once every ten seconds. When you go down she comes for you: she has twelve seconds to reach you, and two and a half seconds beside you gets you back up on half health, with a moment before anything can hurt you. She can do that once a minute (her line on the HUD counts it down). If she is down herself, or has used her revive too recently, going down ends the run. She still shoots, and you can still get her up by standing next to her. Two of the level-up upgrades are for her medic work: bigger heals, and healing and reviving more often. Her numbers are in `MEDIC` in `js/actors.js`.

There are two arenas. **Switch to the Relay Station** on the survival title screen (or `?mode=survival&region=2`) plays it indoors, with every door open. The station is four rooms in a loop: the gate room, the service corridor, the cooling hall, and a freight bay that runs south of all three and joins the gate room to the hall through two blast doors wide enough for a walker. Machines come in through the gate, out of the vents in the corridor's alcoves and from the edges of the hall and the bay. Every minute or so the power fails for about twenty seconds: emergency lights only, the blast doors seal either the cooling hall or the gate room off from the rest, and the machines come faster until it is back. Each arena keeps its own best time.

The numbers that set the pressure (time between groups, group size, how many machines at once, when walkers start) are in one block at the top of `js/survival.js`. An arena can replace any of them: the station's, and the timing of its power failures, are at the bottom of `js/region2.js`.

## Region 02 test section (paused)

The Relay Station, an indoor region on the other side of the gate, was started before the switch to survival. Its test section (the gate room, a service corridor and the cooling hall) still plays: add `?region=2` to the address, e.g. http://localhost:8000/?region=2. It saves to its own slot in this browser only, so it never touches your Dead City save. The freight bay added for survival is behind blast doors that stay shut here. It isn't being extended as a level; its pieces are planned for reuse as survival arenas (see step 8 below).

## Saving

Inside Claude, progress saves to your account. Run anywhere else, it saves to that browser's local storage instead, automatically.

## Controls

Right-click to walk somewhere (hold to keep steering; right-click Mara, the stash or the gate to walk over and use it) · hold left-click to fire: the gun locks on to the nearest threat in range by itself (point at an enemy, or a part of a walker, to aim there instead; with nothing in range it fires toward the cursor) · guns never need reloading · S stop · T next part · Space dodge toward the cursor · Q swap · E use · I pack · C command Mara · M mute · Esc menu. On a phone the game plays in landscape: drag your left thumb anywhere on the left side for a joystick, and hold Fire to shoot (it locks on to the nearest threat by itself, or tap an enemy to lock on to it). Dodge, Swap and Use buttons appear on screen.

## Where it's heading: survival

Relay Gate is changing from a level-by-level campaign to an endless survival game. Machines keep coming, the pressure climbs, bosses arrive as you level up, and the run lasts until you die. The combat already suits this: the gun locks on by itself while you steer, and the loot system was built to reward kill after kill.

### Decided

- **Endless.** No set length or final wave: you play until you die. The score is how long you lasted, with your level and kills alongside it.
- **Mara is in every run, as the medic.** Her job is keeping you alive: healing you and getting you back up when you go down.
- **Two ways to get stronger, both in the same run:** loot that drops from kills (weapons and armour, as now), and an upgrade you choose each time you level up.

### How a run plays

- **Waves:** machines arrive from off-screen. They come faster and in bigger groups over time, and the mix gets harder: drones and husks first, then walkers.
- **Levelling:** kills give XP. Each level pauses the game and offers three upgrades to pick one from, e.g. damage, fire rate, move speed, health, dodge cooldown, a new perk, or something for Mara.
- **Bosses:** a Hunter Walker every few levels, and the Warden at milestone levels. The Warden already has phases and calls in drones and husks, which suits this.
- **Loot:** drops and the pack work as they do now, so you can swap guns and armour mid-run.
- **Death:** an end card with time survived, level, kills, and your best run.

### Build order

Each step should be playable before the next one starts:

1. **Survival mode on the Dead City map.** *(First version in, untuned.)* Add a spawner that brings waves in from off-screen and ramps up over time. This is the step that shows whether the idea is fun.
2. **XP and level-up choices.** *(In, untuned.)* Add an XP bar, a level-up screen with three upgrades, and the upgrades themselves.
3. **Bosses on level milestones.** *(In, untuned.)* Walkers every few levels, and the Warden at the big ones.
4. **WASD movement on desktop.** Right-click to walk is awkward for dodging round a crowd. Phones already use a joystick.
5. **Mara as the medic.** *(In, untuned.)* Make healing and revives her main job, and give her upgrade options of her own.
6. **End card and best run.** Show the run's numbers, save your record, and add Run again.
7. **Something that carries over between runs** (gear, unlocks or a starting choice), once the core run is fun on its own.
8. **More arenas.** *(The station is in as a second arena, untuned.)* Reuse the Region 02 work: the wall generator and power zones can make indoor arenas where, for example, the lights die every few waves.

### Things to watch

- **How many enemies at once.** Every machine is a fully modelled, individually animated actor with its own materials and shadows. Dozens on screen is fine; hundreds (a Vampire Survivors-style swarm) would mean rebuilding how enemies are drawn and updated. Plan for fewer, tougher enemies.
- **Balance is the main job now.** Spawn rates, the difficulty ramp and boss timing are the game, and they get tuned by playing, not up front.
- **Walkers need room.** They walk round cover instead of through it, on a map of the ground that leaves space for something their size. Where no gap is wide enough (the station's 3 m corridor) there is no way through for them; in the station they go round by the freight bay.
- **Drones indoors.** Outdoors they fly over everything. Indoors they now go through the doorways like anything on foot, and are held to a person's width so they can cross from room to room, which lets their rotors clip a wall now and then.

### Still open

- **Where a run starts.** Dead City starts in the gate plaza and the station in the gate room, both with the gate stuck open. Neither has been compared with anywhere else.
- **Does Mara still shoot,** or only heal and revive? For now she does all three.
- **What carries over between runs,** if anything, and how much.
- **What happens to the Dead City campaign** once survival works: keep it as a separate mode, or retire it.
- **Indoor ambience** for station arenas: machine hum, fans, electrical buzz (the current recordings are all outdoor).
